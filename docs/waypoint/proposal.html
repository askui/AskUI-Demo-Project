Three things go wrong when a founder travels: promises get dropped, proximity goes unnoticed, and good relationships quietly decay. All three are the same missing join.

What's actually broken

One complaint, three different failures

They need separate machinery, so it's worth naming them separately before designing anything.

Dropped promise

A commitment with no trigger

"let me know when you're in the south bay and we'll grab coffee"

You meant it. It lives inside a message thread and nothing ever turned it into an object that could fire later.

Invisible proximity

A graph with no geography

4,100 connections · 180 in the Bay Area · 9 worth a coffee this week

Your network has no location attached. LinkedIn will never tell you "these nine of yours are here right now".

Decay

Warmth with no clock

Two great calls in 2025. Then fourteen months of nothing.

Nothing measures staleness against relationship value, and nothing knows when re-contacting is cheap — same city, same week.

The core object

The Trip Brief

A trip is a place plus a date range. The brief turns it into a ranked list of cards. Every card answers who, why now, what's the evidence, and what do I send — and shows the arithmetic that ranked it.

Ranking

Explainable arithmetic, not a model

The user has to be able to see why a card ranked where it did. The first inexplicable suggestion is where trust dies, so the ranking stays hand-tunable code.

score  =  value  ×  timeliness  ×  feasibility

Value

  • Promise — an explicit unreturned invitation. Strongest signal in the system.
  • ICP — company and title against your fit profile.
  • Investor — stage, thesis, check size vs. your next raise.
  • Partner — integrators, bench vendors, tooling ecosystems.
  • Relationship — warmth with no commercial angle. Lower, never zero.

Timeliness

  • Promise age, decayed — old ones surface as overdue rather than dropping off.
  • Staleness measured against your historic cadence with that person.
  • Live triggers: job change, funding round, a post about a problem you solve.

Feasibility

  • Travel time from that day's anchor — not straight-line distance. SoMa to the Peninsula is a two-hour commitment and the card should say so.
  • Do you have a real gap in the calendar.
  • Are they reachable — recent activity, past response rate.

Dismissals are training data, so they're three buttons, not one: not relevant, not now, and wrong person update different weights.

Feasibility check

The LinkedIn problem, honestly

LinkedIn is the whole product and LinkedIn does not want to give you this data. Any design that hand-waves here is a design that doesn't ship.

PathWhat you getThe catch
Data exportStart here Connections file plus the complete message archive — which is exactly where the forgotten promises are. Official and ToS-clean, but manual and roughly monthly. Carries no location field, which the whole product pivots on.
GojberryAlready yours Live LinkedIn threads and outbound sending, for conversations that flow through it. Only covers what's in that inbox — a live channel, not the historical archive.

Official partner APIsNot a path Effectively nothing relevant. No connection list, no message read access.

And the location gap

Connections export with no location, so the pivot field has to be manufactured in three tiers: enrich the top few hundred by score only (never all 4,000 — that's real money), infer from company offices, past meeting locations and message timestamps, and correct by hand with one tap that sticks forever. Every location carries a confidence, and a weak one reads as "likely in SF" rather than a false certainty.

Everything else you already have connected: calendar and flight confirmations for trip detection, meeting transcripts for what you promised out loud, and the CRM for pipeline context and write-back.

Sequence

Build the payoff first

Phase 1
~2 weeks

"We found 23 open invitations in your history. Eleven are still warm."

Import the export, run promise extraction over the entire archive, and ship exactly one screen: every open promise, ranked by warmth × age, each with its original quote and a draft reply. No trips, no map, no geography. This is useful on its own — and if this screen isn't compelling, stop and don't build the rest.

Phase 2

Trips

Manual trip creation, the place hierarchy that makes "South Bay" match a trip to "SF", location enrichment for the top-ranked few hundred, and the full ranked brief with drafts. Calendar read for real free slots.

Phase 3

Ambient

Trips detected from flight mail and calendar. A continuous sweep so new promises are captured as they arrive. One pre-trip brief and one daily digest while travelling. Meeting transcripts in, interactions written back to the CRM.

Phase 4

Sharpen

Fit profiles editable in plain language. Live signals — job changes, funding, posts. Weights tuned by your dismissals. Live LinkedIn read, if you've decided you want it.

Before building

Five decisions that are yours

Live LinkedIn read, or monthly exports?

Freshness against account risk. Recommendation: exports through Phase 3 — it changes nothing before Phase 4, so the decision can wait until you've seen whether the product earns it.

Personal tool, or product?

Everything above is designed single-user. Recommendation: stay single-user until Phase 3 ships — going multi-tenant makes both the privacy architecture and the LinkedIn access story materially harder.

How much enrichment do you buy?

Recommendation: top 300 by score, plus anyone with an open promise, plus on demand. Most of a 4,000-person network will never rank into a brief.

How loud is it?

Recommendation: one pre-trip brief, one daily digest while travelling, nothing else. An app that pings you about people is an app you mute in week two.

Whose voice do the drafts use?

They need a style sample from your real sent messages, or every draft reads like a sales sequence and you rewrite all of them — at which point the feature is worse than nothing. And nothing ever auto-sends.

One more thing

This system will hold every private message you've exchanged with thousands of people

Including their words, which they did not consent to hand to a vendor. That makes a few things non-negotiable rather than nice-to-have: single-tenant or self-hosted storage, only the relevant thread window ever sent to a model, message bodies encrypted at rest with just the derived objects in the hot path, and deleting a person deleting their messages — not just their row.
